# Clarinet (in B♭; in A)

register: 5000–8900
- 5000–6400 low (chalumeau): dark, hollow and full; plays very softly with ease
- 6500–6800 throat tones: paler and less resonant; moving between 6800 and 6900 (the break) is awkward in fast passages
- 6900–8200 clarion: clear and brilliant
- 8300–8900 high: bright, more effortful
expanded_register: 8900–9400 very high: shrill, loud, difficult.
transposing: yes: the clarinet in B♭ sounds 200 below written. The clarinet in A sounds 300 below written and reaches down to 4900; otherwise the same. Every value here is sounding pitch; write sounding pitch.
clefs: G
techniques: the woodwind techniques (woodwinds/_family.md), plus:
- the widest dynamic range of the woodwinds: it can begin from and fade into silence in every register
- very agile: fast scales, arpeggios and wide leaps across the registers
- real glissando upward in the clarion and high registers (above about 7000), up to an octave or more ("gliss"); elsewhere bends of about a semitone ("gliss": ±100)
- slap tongue: "text": "slap"
- multiphonics: many, softer than the oboe's

## Harmonics
The cylindrical bore overblows at the twelfth and gives only the odd partials.
Partial n sounds at the fingered note + 1200·log2(n): 3 → +1902, 5 → +2786,
7 → +3369, 9 → +3804. From the low fingerings 5000–6400; from 5000: 6902 7786
8369 8804. The clarion register is itself the third partial of the low
register. The real pitch can differ by a few cents. Harmonic fingerings give
softer, hollower versions of these pitches.

Write the sounding pitch with "art": "harmonic".
