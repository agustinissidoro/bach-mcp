# Oboe

register: 5800–8800
- 5800–6200 low: thick, nasal and loud; very soft playing is hard
- 6200–8100 middle: the characteristic penetrating, singing sound
- 8100–8800 high: thinner and more strained
expanded_register: 8800–9300 very high: difficult, mostly in solo and contemporary writing. Some oboes have a low key down to 5700.
transposing: no
clefs: G
techniques: the woodwind techniques (woodwinds/_family.md), plus:
- uses little air, so long phrases are possible (10–20 s), but the player must also breathe out: gaps are still needed
- carries easily through the ensemble; slow, expressive melody is its strength
- crisp staccato; wide fast leaps are less fluent than on the flute or clarinet
- bends of about a quarter-tone to a semitone ("gliss": ±50 to ±100)
- multiphonics: many, often harsh and loud

## Harmonics
The conical bore overblows at the octave and gives all partials. Partial n
sounds at the fingered note + 1200·log2(n): 2 → +1200, 3 → +1902, 4 → +2400.
From low fingerings 5800–6400; from 5800: 7000 7702 8200. The real pitch can
differ by a few cents. Rare; softer and more veiled than the normal notes.

Write the sounding pitch with "art": "harmonic".
