# Violin

register: 5500–9600
- 5500–6900 (G and D strings): warm; dark and intense on the G string
- 6900–8400 (A and E strings): bright, the main singing register
- 8400–9600: brilliant and thinner; fast passages still easy
expanded_register: 9600–10500 in solo writing: thin, hard to tune, best in slow passages. Natural harmonics reach this register more easily (see Harmonics).
transposing: no
clefs: G; G8va for passages that stay above about 9600
techniques: the string-family techniques (strings/_family.md), plus:
- open strings 5500 6200 6900 7600
- the most agile string instrument: fast scales, arpeggios and wide leaps in every register
- double stops: thirds, sixths and octaves are idiomatic; tenths are a stretch
- pizz. is short and dry above about 8400

## Harmonics
A natural harmonic is a partial of an open string, played by touching the
string lightly at a node. Partial n sounds at the open string + 1200·log2(n):
2 → +1200, 3 → +1902, 4 → +2400, 5 → +2786, 6 → +3102.

```
string   partial 2     3     4     5     6
5500              6700  7402  7900  8286  8602
6200              7400  8102  8600  8986  9302
6900              8100  8802  9300  9686 10002
7600              8800  9502 10000 10386 10702
```

Partials 2 to 4 are secure, 5 and 6 softer and less sure. An artificial
harmonic is a stopped note S with a second finger touching above it: touching a
fourth above sounds S + 2400 (the usual one), a fifth above S + 1902, a major
third above S + 2786. Harmonics are soft, pure and glassy, and carry little
weight in loud music.

Write the sounding pitch with "art": "harmonic"; bach plays the written pitch,
so the score sounds right. A harmonic glissando (the finger sliding lightly
along the string) is a fast run of these partials, about 40–80 ms apart.
