# Bowed strings (violin, viola, cello, double bass)

Four strings, tuned in fifths (the double bass in fourths), bowed (arco) or
plucked (pizz.). Fretless: any pitch between semitones is playable exactly, so
quarter-tones and other microtones are written as plain midicents.

techniques:
- arco (bowed, the default): annotation "arco" to return from another technique
- pizzicato: annotation "pizz."; a plucked note that decays: lower notes ring longer
- left-hand pizzicato: articulation lefthandpizzicato
- snap (Bartók) pizzicato, string slapping the fingerboard: annotation "snap pizz."
- sul ponticello, bow near the bridge, glassy and rich in high partials: annotation "sul pont."
- sul tasto, bow over the fingerboard, soft and veiled: annotation "sul tasto"
- back to the normal bow position: annotation "ord."
- flautando, light fast bow, airy: annotation "flautando"
- col legno battuto, struck with the wood of the bow: annotation "col legno batt."
- col legno tratto, drawn with the wood: annotation "col legno tratto"
- mute on / off: annotation "con sord." / "senza sord."; it takes a few seconds to put on or take off
- bowed tremolo, fast repeated strokes on one note: articulation tremolo3; measured: articulation tremolo2 or tremolo1
- trill: articulation trill
- natural and artificial harmonics: articulation harmonic on the sounding pitch (see each instrument's Harmonics)
- glissando: a glissando on the note; smooth along one string, any interval
- bow direction: articulation downbowing / upbowing
- overpressure, scratch tone, noise: annotation "overpressure"
- jeté / ricochet, the bow bouncing: annotation "jeté"
- bowing behind the bridge, tapping the body: notehead cross with annotation "behind the bridge" or "tap body"

articulations: staccato and staccatissimo are short separate bow strokes;
portato (tenuto) is full length with a slight separation; accent and martellato
(marcato) are a stronger bow attack. There are no slurs in this score: for legato, let each
note last until the next onset.

constraints:
- double stops: two notes at once on two adjacent strings, one note per string;
  the lower note can be no lower than the lower string's open pitch.
- three or four notes bowed together are broken (played as a fast arpeggio from
  the bottom), except when short and loud; in pizz. they sound together.
- open strings ring longer, cannot take vibrato and cannot slide.
